Unit 2 – Tourism Distribution Chain & E-business (exam Notes)
Tourism Distribution Chain & E-Business in Tourism
________________________________________
Big Picture: Why the Tourism Distribution Chain Matters
Tourism is one of the most important global industries.
Key facts highlighted in the chapter:
• international and domestic tourism generate up to 10% of global GDP
• many small countries depend heavily on tourism income
• the industry is globalized, dominated by multinational companies
• the online environment does not change tourism itself, only how business is done
This means e-business is a tool, not a different tourism industry. 
________________________________________
The Tourism Ecosystem – How Everything Connects
The tourism ecosystem links:
• tourist-generating regions
• tourist transit routes
• tourist destination regions
Within this system, different actors interact:
• travelers (domestic and international)
• tourism service providers
• intermediaries
• public authorities
Tourism also creates:
• economic impacts
• social impacts
• environmental impacts
________________________________________
Tourism Distribution System – Core Idea
The tourism distribution system explains how tourism products reach the customer.
It connects:
• primary suppliers
• intermediaries
• digital platforms
• final customers
E-business platforms make this process faster, global, and automated. 
________________________________________
Travel Agency Terminology – Essential Definitions
Travel Agency
A travel agency can legally act as:
• a tour operator
• a travel agent
• or both, depending on licenses
________________________________________
Tour Operator
A tour operator:
• creates its own tourism product
• combines services into a package trip
• acts as an organizer or wholesaler
________________________________________
Travel Agent
A travel agent:
• does not create products
• sells products created by others
• acts only as an intermediary
They earn income mainly through commission.
Forms:
• traditional (offline) travel agents
• online travel agents (OTA)
________________________________________
Main Participants in the Tourism Distribution Chain
Primary Suppliers
These create the core tourism product:
• accommodation providers
• airlines
• transport companies
• cruise companies
• attractions and activities
________________________________________
Tour Operators and Wholesalers
Wholesalers or bedbanks:
• buy services in bulk
• work with net rates
• focus on B2B relationships
• rarely sell directly to individuals
Their platforms are often password-protected. 
________________________________________
Travel Agents and OTAs
Travel agents:
• sell tourism services to customers
• work on commission
• exist both offline and online
OTAs perform the same function digitally.
Examples:
• Booking platforms
• Online travel agencies
________________________________________
Event Management Companies
Event companies are also tourism intermediaries when they organize:
• weddings
• festivals
• concerts
• business events
They arrange:
• accommodation
• transport
• food and beverage
• entertainment
________________________________________
Global Distribution Systems (GDS)
A Global Distribution System (GDS) is:
• a global network
• connecting travel suppliers with travel agencies
• enabling automated bookings
Main users:
• airlines
• hotels
• car rental companies
________________________________________
Software Systems on the Supplier Side
PMS and CRS
Property Management System (PMS) or Central Reservation System (CRS):
• internal systems used by suppliers
• manage reservations, availability, and operations
These systems do not sell directly to customers but connect to other platforms.
________________________________________
Booking Engine
A booking engine:
• allows online booking on the supplier’s own website
• supports direct sales
________________________________________
Switch Companies
Switch companies:
• connect different CRS systems with GDS
• allow information to flow between platforms
________________________________________
Channel Manager
A channel manager:
• manages room rates and availability
• synchronizes all sales channels in real time
• reduces overbooking risk
Modern channel managers often use AI.
________________________________________
Meta-Search Engines
Meta-search engines:
• compare offers from multiple OTAs and supplier websites
• show prices side-by-side
• simplify customer decision-making
________________________________________
Communication Channels – Important Clarification
Communication channels include:
• media and social media
• blogs and forums
• email and telephone

Key exam point:
These are not part of the distribution system.
They are:
• marketing tools
• promotional channels
When primary suppliers use them directly, it becomes direct sales.

Unit 4–5 – Relationships & Cash Flows In The Tourism Distribution Chain 
Relationships & Cash Flows in the Tourism Distribution Chain
________________________________________
Big Picture: Why Relationships and Cash Flows Matter
Tourism distribution is not only about selling services, but about:
• contractual relationships between participants
• responsibility for pricing and payments
• timing and direction of cash flows
________________________________________
Core Business Models in Tourism Distribution
Two dominant models define relationships and money flows:
	Merchant Model – How It Works
• suppliers negotiate net rates in advance with tour operators or intermediaries
• contracts include blocked rooms (inventory / contingent / allotment)
• intermediaries apply a mark-up (margin or profit)
• clients pay the intermediary, not the supplier
The client receives a voucher:
• confirms prepaid services
• does not show the price
________________________________________
Merchant Model – Supplier Perspective
Advantages
• guaranteed room occupancy in advance
• predictable net revenues
• easier price list planning
Disadvantages
• lower bargaining power
• lower revenues due to net rates
• difficult control of rate parity
• delayed payments (after guest departure)
________________________________________
Merchant Model – Intermediary Perspective
Advantages
• freedom to define mark-up
• strong bargaining power due to volume
• ability to create package trips
Disadvantages
• complex legal documentation
• negotiations with many suppliers
• possible advance payments affecting cash flow
________________________________________
AI in the Merchant Model
AI supports merchant intermediaries through:
• demand and inventory forecasting
• automated contract analysis using NLP
• AI-driven dynamic pricing and margin optimization
• intelligent dynamic packaging of services
Example logic:
AI predicts demand months ahead and adjusts purchase volumes and prices automatically. fileciteturn3file0
________________________________________
	Agent Model – How It Works
In the agent model:
• suppliers set the final selling price
• agents sell at the supplier’s price
• suppliers pay a commission to the agent
• commission is usually a percentage of the selling price
Payment options:
• client pays supplier directly
• or client pays agent and receives a booking confirmation with full price details
________________________________________
Agent Model – Supplier Perspective
Advantages
• higher revenues
• control over pricing
• easier rate parity management
• diversified intermediary network
Disadvantages
• complex management of many intermediaries
________________________________________
Agent Model – Intermediary Perspective
Advantages
• simpler transactions
• rarely handle payments
• attractive and transparent for clients
• simpler legal relations
Disadvantages
• no control over prices
• competition with both other agents and suppliers
________________________________________
AI in the Agent Model
AI is widely used by OTAs through:
• personalized recommendation systems
• sentiment analysis of reviews
• AI chatbots and virtual travel assistants
• predictive analytics for commission optimization
AI improves conversion rates without changing the core agent logic. fileciteturn3file0
________________________________________
When Each Model Is Used
Merchant model is typically used by:
• tour operators
• B2B platforms
• package trip organizers
Agent model is typically used by:
• online travel agencies (OTAs)
________________________________________
Cash Flows in the Tourism Distribution Chain
Cash flows depend on:
• booking channel
• business model used
• number of intermediaries
Typical scenarios illustrated in the slides:
• direct booking at hotel
• booking via travel agent
• booking via tour operator
• multi-level distribution chains

Each participant receives only their agreed share:
• supplier – net rate
• intermediary – margin or commission
________________________________________
Direct Booking Scenario
Characteristics:
• client pays hotel directly
• hotel issues invoice or receipt
• no intermediaries involved
This provides maximum control for the supplier.
________________________________________
Booking via Agent or Tour Operator
• agent earns commission
• tour operator earns margin
• vouchers replace invoices for clients in merchant models
________________________________________
Intermediaries vs Infomediaries
Intermediaries:
• participate directly in distribution
• influence pricing and payments
Infomediaries:
• provide information only
• do not handle payments
• support decision-making
________________________________________
Ethical and Exam-Relevant Discussion Points
• ethical issues of AI-driven price discrimination
• responsibility allocation in AI-enhanced models
• risk management differences between merchant and agent models
________________________________________
