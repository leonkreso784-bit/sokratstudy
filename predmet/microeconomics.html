What three steps make up the theory of consumer behavior?
1. Consumer preferences — what the consumer likes (indifference curves) 2. Budget constraints — what the consumer can afford 3. Consumer choices — combining the two to maximize satisfaction
How is an individual demand curve derived from consumer theory?
By tracing the optimal quantity of a good as its price changes (the price-consumption path). At every point on the demand curve the consumer is maximizing utility, but the level of utility changes as you move along it.
Define probability, payoff, and expected value.
• Probability — the likelihood a given outcome occurs. • Payoff — the value associated with a possible outcome. • Expected value — a probability-weighted average of the payoffs: \( E(X) = \sum_i p_i x_i \)
What three decisions make up the theory of the firm’s production?
1. Production technology — how inputs turn into output 2. Cost constraints — the prices of labor, capital and other inputs 3. Input choices — how much of each input to use
Economic cost vs. accounting cost — what is the difference?
Accounting cost counts explicit out-of-pocket expenses (and depreciation). Economic cost also includes the OPPORTUNITY cost of all resources — e.g. the salary the owner gives up, or the return on capital used in the business.
How is profit defined, and what does a firm maximize?
Profit is total revenue minus total cost: \( \pi = TR - TC \) The firm chooses the output level that makes this difference as large as possible.
