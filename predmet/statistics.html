Examples from the material

Distinguish a population from a sample, and a parameter from a statistic.
A population is the collection of ALL items of interest (size \(N\)); a sample is an observed subset (size \(n\)). A parameter is a numerical characteristic of a POPULATION; a statistic is a numerical characteristic of a SAMPLE.
Define the three measures of central tendency.
• Mean — the arithmetic average: \( \bar{x} = \dfrac{\sum x_i}{n} \) • Median — the middle value of ordered data (50% above, 50% below) • Mode — the most frequently occurring value (may be none or several)
Define random experiment, sample space and event.
A random experiment is a process with two or more possible outcomes, unknown in advance. The sample space \(S\) is the set of ALL possible outcomes. An event is any subset of the sample space.
What is a discrete random variable?
A variable representing a numerical outcome of a random experiment that can take only a COUNTABLE number of values — e.g. the number of defective items, claims, or arrivals.
What is a continuous random variable?
A variable that can take ANY value within an interval (weight, time, temperature). Because there are infinitely many possible values, the probability of any single exact value is zero.
What is a sampling distribution?
The distribution of all possible values of a statistic (e.g. the sample mean) for a given sample size taken from a population. Different samples give different statistic values, and the sampling distribution describes that variability.
Distinguish an estimator from an estimate, and a point estimate from an interval estimate.

What is a statistical hypothesis, and what are H0 and H1?
A hypothesis is a claim about a population parameter. The NULL hypothesis \(H_0\) is the status quo (always contains \(=,\ \le,\ \ge\)); the ALTERNATIVE \(H_1\) is its opposite (\(\ne,\ \)) and is what the researcher tries to support.
